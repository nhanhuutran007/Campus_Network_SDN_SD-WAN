\section*{\fontsize{16pt}{15pt}\selectfont BUILDING A CAMPUS NETWORK \\USING SDN AND SD-WAN \\ABSTRACT}

\begin{itemize}
    \item[-] \textbf{Research issues:}

    Universities with several campuses need a network that is highly available, secure, centrally managed and able to connect all sites reliably. Traditional networks are configured device by device, lack a global view of the network and depend on a single WAN link. This project designs, implements and verifies a multi-campus network that combines a hierarchical campus design with Software-Defined Networking (SDN) and Software-Defined WAN (SD-WAN). The report includes 5 chapters:
        \begin{itemize}
        \item[•] Chapter 1 --- Introduction and Project Overview
        \item[•] Chapter 2 --- Theoretical Background
        \item[•] Chapter 3 --- Proposed Campus Network Design
        \item[•] Chapter 4 --- Implementation, Testing and Evaluation
        \item[•] Chapter 5 --- Conclusion and Future Work
        \end{itemize}
    \item[-] \textbf{Approach:}
        \begin{itemize}
        \item[•] Theory: Study VLAN, RSTP, EtherChannel, VRRP, OSPF, BGP, stateful firewalls and DMZ, OpenFlow 1.3, Open vSwitch, Ryu, ONOS and the Cisco SD-WAN (Viptela) architecture with OMP, TLOC, BFD, PKI and centralized policies.
        \item[•] Practice: Build a 67-node lab on EVE-NG with a main campus (Site 100), three branch campuses (Sites 200, 300, 400), an SD-WAN control site (Site 900) and two simulated service providers (Internet and MPLS); verify every function layer by layer.
    \end{itemize}
    \item[-] \textbf{How to solve problems:}
    The main campus uses a Core--Distribution--Access design: two Cisco core switches provide VRRP gateways, OSPF and DHCP relay; an ASAv Active/Standby firewall pair protects the DMZ (Web, Mail) and the server farm (DHCP, DNS, Syslog). All six layer-2 switches are Open vSwitch instances controlled by a custom Ryu application (OpenFlow 1.3) that performs VLAN-aware MAC learning, centralized loop prevention, a dedicated management tree for in-band control, distribution failover, centralized ACLs, a REST API and port-load monitoring, while ONOS runs in parallel for topology visualization. Branches use a Firewall-as-Core design. The sites are connected by Cisco SD-WAN 20.10.1 with eight vEdge routers over two transports, a BGP underlay, PKI authentication, IPsec overlay and vSmart policies (SLA classes, Application-Aware Routing, data and control policies).
    \item[-] \textbf{Results achieved:}
    18 of 22 test cases passed. DHCP works at all sites; the six switches stay connected to the controller and recover automatically after restarts without broadcast storms; all eight vEdge routers joined the fabric with every BFD session up; hosts at different campuses communicate over the overlay and use the internal DNS, HTTPS web and mail services; traffic switched to the Internet transport in about 2 seconds when MPLS failed. Remaining limitations include DHCP not being maintained during distribution failover, the single SDN controller and the lack of direct Internet access for users.
\end{itemize}
